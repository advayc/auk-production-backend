Adobe vs AuK benchmark

Twelve recordings with different noise problems, each in three versions: the original, Adobe Podcast Enhance (free tier, captured Nov 17, 2026), and the AuK candidate (current pipeline at forced full strength, wet 1.0). The production automatic decision would pick a lighter blend on 6 of these 12 inputs (strength audit, Nov 16), so these players show the candidate at maximum cleanup, not what auto mode currently ships. Which AuK version is best is not established - that is a listening call, and it is what this page is for. "AuK fuller-tone EXPERIMENTAL" players are collected in their own section below the grid, separate from the main comparison (the client version is the one the owner listened to and said sounded better; the rest are new and unreviewed): the grid variant is the same tone chain with its compressor threshold set relative to each clip's own level instead of one fixed value, a repair of the original chain's uneven cross-clip behavior - it reproduces the heard client version by calibration and measurably tracks Adobe's per-clip dynamics more evenly (mean LRA distance to Adobe on the 5 comparable clips: 1.66 -> 1.24 LU); it is not adopted and not a quality claim. 5 of the grid players were added Nov 19 from durably regenerated premasters (sha256-verified transfer, rebuilds correlate r > 0.9999 with the published candidates). Built Nov 17, 2026. Related: client recording deep-dive (breath analysis, earlier experiments), full-vs-light strength pairs.

Level-matched playersAll players on this page are level-matched by default: within each recording, every version is attenuated to the same integrated loudness (the quietest version's level). Attenuation only - nothing is amplified, so peaks never grow and tonal and dynamics differences are preserved. Raw (unmatched) files are linked under each player, and the measurement table below reports raw levels.

Raw loudness differs a lot between versions - Adobe's free tier lands quieter (I -18 to -24) with heavy true-peak margin (-4.5 to -10.8), while the AuK candidate sits near -16 LUFS with peaks under -0.9. That gap is exactly why the players above are level-matched. Quiet floors differ most on the noisiest recordings (moto, cr7, tucci, intentional): the unblended full-strength candidate drives pause floors far down (e.g. moto bed -94 dBFS vs Adobe -45). Whether that sounds clean or hollow is a listening question, not a numbers question.

Integrity checks (pass/fail, all rows pass): raw durations agree within 0.05 s on every triple (max delta 0.050 s); matched renders are sample-rate-exact with their raw sources (duration delta 0.000 s); matched levels verified against their targets on the encoded mp3 (51 of 52 within 0.04 LU, max residual +0.04/-0.04; one noted exception at -0.45); matched renders are attenuation-only (max applied gain 0.0 dB, max true peak -2.4 dBTP; the listened fuller-tone matched player verified at its row target of -22.9 LUFS with -3.6 dB applied); no processed output clips - every Adobe and AuK file keeps at least 0.9 dB true-peak headroom. Some originals themselves hit 0 dBTP (they are raw uploads). Client row checks are on the deep-dive page.

Client pause windows, incl. tone experiments

Ten inter-word pauses on the client recording, RMS dBFS per window. The fuller-tone experiments v1 and v2 are experimental variants, built to test one hypothesis: that the tone and dynamics of the owner-supplied Adobe reference are achievable on the AuK candidate. Neither is selected or production-approved. v1 applies tone/dynamics shaping to the candidate's mp3 after mastering (post-master processing of a lossy source). v2 applies a refined chain before mastering, on a reconstruction of the run's premaster from its 23 durable per-chunk exports, then passes through the same production mastering stage. The reconstruction is validated end-to-end: re-rendering the full chain reproduces the run record's final measurements exactly (I -19.05 LUFS / TP -1.0 dBTP / LRA 8.9 LU) and matches the published candidate at 0.99993 sample correlation (the residual difference is consistent with mp3 coding). v2 is the cleaner test of the hypothesis.

Overall levels: v1: I -19.04 LUFS (matched to candidate), TP -1.03 dBTP, LRA 4.6 LU. v2: I -18.84, TP -1.00, LRA 4.2 (candidate: 8.9; owner-supplied Adobe reference: 3.5). Loudness-normalized band shape vs that reference: v2 is the closest of the three on 6 of 9 bands and stays within +/-2.8 dB from 60 Hz to 12 kHz (worst gaps: candidate +4.3 dB at 2-4 kHz, v1 +5.3 dB at 2-4 kHz, v2 +1.8 dB at 2-4 kHz). In the 20-60 Hz sub band v2 sits +3.7 dB above the reference while the candidate sits 3.0 dB below it; the two Adobe files themselves disagree in this band, and on speech it is mostly non-speech content, so it is noted rather than chased. Compression lifts some quieter pauses above the candidate; the loud breath at 3:41 stays at source level on all variants. A fixed chain tuned on this recording does not match Adobe's per-clip behavior on the other 11 recordings (Adobe's own measured dynamics range from LRA 3.1 to 22.0 across them), so this stays a client-scoped experiment, not a global change.

What the measurements say (diagnostics, not listening)

Ten inter-word pause windows, measured the same way on the Adobe output and the candidate; a side counts as cleaner only when it is more than 0.5 dB lower. Adobe is cleaner in 5 of 10 windows, the candidate in 3 of 10, and 2 are tied. The windows where Adobe is cleaner mostly hold discrete sounds - breaths and movement - by 2.5-8 dB. On true stationary pauses the candidate goes much quieter than Adobe (e.g. the 3:34 pause: candidate -85.5 dB, Adobe -44.4 dB). One loud breath at 3:41 is kept at near-original level by both.

These are pause-level measurements only. They describe background level in specific windows; they are not quality scores and they say nothing about how the voice itself sounds. An automatic machine-vs-machine transcript check also flags about 3% word difference on the candidate - a place to listen (the 2:30 excerpt above), not confirmed word loss. Signal-integrity checks pass on all three versions (sample-exact duration, no clipping, level targets within ceiling).

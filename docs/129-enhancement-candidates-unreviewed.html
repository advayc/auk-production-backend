Technical notes · settings, measurements, method

Settings glossary. "Enhance, full strength" = enhance instruction at full strength, unblended (the processed output only, none of the original mixed back in). "Denoise, full strength" = denoise instruction at full strength, unblended. "Enhance + 15% original" / "+ 8% original" = full-strength enhance output blended with that share of the original recording. "Standard output (default settings)" = the pipeline's output with its default settings; our earlier comparison baseline.

Provenance. Motorcycle and client candidates processed Nov 12, 2026 through the production voice-enhancement pipeline (repository commit f6e8c85, seed 42); follow-up client variants Nov 16, 2026 (commit 20b837a, seed 42, same harness). Per-candidate settings are recorded in the run manifests. Full-length audio here is lossy MP3 for review (the Adobe output is served as supplied, 128 kbps); lossless masters and the measurement manifest are retained privately.

Measured comparison (client recording). Per-window levels in 10 inter-word windows, lower = cleaner, a side counts as cleaner only by >0.5 dB per window: the candidate is cleaner in 3 of 10 windows (e.g. the 214.1s pause: candidate -85.5 dB, Adobe -44.4 dB, standard output -23.3 dB), Adobe in 5 of 10 (mostly windows holding discrete sounds - breaths, movement - by 2.5-8 dB), 2 tied. One loud breath (221.4-222.2s) is kept at near-original level by both. The Adobe master is quieter and more compressed overall (measured LRA 3.5 vs 8.9-9.4 for ours), which changes its overall character. Window RMS measured in dBFS; overall figures via ffmpeg loudnorm.

Nov 16 follow-up variants. An aggressive denoise variant and an adaptive-strength variant of the client recording were produced and measured the same way. On this recording the adaptive variant's output matched the candidate's within 0.1 dB on every measured window, and the denoise variant measured between the candidate and Adobe on deep pauses; neither closes the discrete-sound gap and neither changes the recommendation. Both are retained privately with their manifests.

Diagnostics are not quality scores. Notes on this page come from automatic speech-to-text word diffs, a speaker-similarity score, and energy measurements. They locate where to listen; they do not score how it sounds. Automatic transcript checks show a comparable number of small word-diff flags across Adobe, the standard output, and the candidates, at different spots; these flags are places to listen, not confirmed word loss. Signal-integrity checks (sample-exact duration, no clipping, level targets) pass on all outputs; transcript review flags are separate from signal integrity and remain open pending listening.

Loudness. Full-length players are mastered to a -1.0 dBTP ceiling but are not loudness-matched to one another - use them for character and artifacts, not volume. Excerpt rows are level-matched within each row (attenuation only, matched down to the quietest variant in that window), so loudness should not bias those A/Bs. References inform listening; no parity or superiority claim is made or implied. Known limit of the Adobe comparison: one recording, and the Adobe file as supplied (128 kbps).
